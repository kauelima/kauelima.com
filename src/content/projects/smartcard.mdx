Smartcard is a digital business card with an analytics layer underneath it. You
share a card; it records that the card was seen, which link was tapped, and who
tapped it. Built at **Bitboundaire**, a near-shore studio taking US startup
products from ideation to launch.

## The problem

A paper business card is a dead end. It transfers information in one direction
and reports nothing back — you hand out fifty at a conference and learn
nothing about which fifty, or which of them ever looked at it again.

Meanwhile the company has the same problem one level up: it cannot see which
of its people are actually generating contacts, or what any of that is worth.

## What I did

The design reframed the card as **the top of a funnel rather than a
credential**, and everything followed from that.

**The card is the product surface.** Photo, name, title, contact details,
company mark, a QR code and a row of link buttons — website, Instagram,
WhatsApp, LinkedIn. It is the thing shared, so it carries the brand and stays
uncluttered.

**Every interaction is an event.** Card visits, link clicks and captured
contacts are counted separately, because they are three different depths of
interest and collapsing them into one number destroys the signal.

**Contacts become leads with a value attached.** The dashboard states the lead
count, and next to it a potential value derived from an average per lead. That
turns a vanity metric into something a manager can act on.

**Comparison is deliberate.** A leaderboard ranks a person against the rest of
their company. On a product whose whole job is networking activity, showing
where you stand is the mechanism that keeps people sharing.

**Two surfaces, one system.** Mobile is for sharing and glanceable stats; web
is for the full lead list, filtering and admin. Both are built from one
component library so they stay the same product.

### The design system

Smartcard's system was built from scratch rather than adapted, and it is the
part of the project with the longest life.

Colour uses **Material Design roles** — primary, container, surface, error and
their `on` pairs, plus interaction, hover and press states — so the token layer
carries meaning and components never hard-code a hex. Components are documented
in **Atomic Design** terms, each with anatomy, properties and states: the button
spec alone covers label grammar, width behaviour, four states, icon slots and
loading.

That specificity is what made the handoff cheap. A component with documented
properties is a conversation that does not need to happen twice.
