Built at **Clube da Entrega** for **McDonald's**, App Restaurante is the
restaurant's side of the delivery operation: the screen the kitchen works from
when orders are arriving faster than they can be cooked.

## The problem

Delivery adds a second, invisible queue to a kitchen that already has one. The
counter queue is self-regulating, because the person waiting is standing right
there. The delivery queue is not: the customer is a name on a screen, and the
cost of an order slipping is paid twenty minutes later by someone else.

At the busiest hours, that produced the failures that matter:

- **No single view of the order.** Received, accepted, in the kitchen, ready,
  collected — each state lived in someone's head or on paper.
- **The urgent order looks like every other order.** A list sorted by arrival
  gives no signal about which one is about to be late.
- **The kitchen cannot stop to read.** Hands are full, the screen is across the
  room, and attention comes in two-second slices.

## What I did

The design treated the app as an **operational instrument, not an inbox**.

**One order, one lifecycle.** Every order is a single object carrying its whole
history from receipt to delivery, so the state is never reconstructed from
memory and the app can answer "where is it" at any moment.

**The queue sorts by urgency, not arrival.** What the kitchen needs is not a
chronological log but an answer to "what next", which means time remaining has
to outrank time received.

**Legible across the room.** Type, contrast and target sizes were set for a
screen glanced at from a few metres away by someone holding something in both
hands, not for a phone held at reading distance.
